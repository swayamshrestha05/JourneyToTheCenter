Ocean floor at 8,849 mThe summit rocks are uplifted ocean sediments full of marine fossils. Winds over 160 km/h, temperatures below −30°C, a third of the air at sea level.

Still rising about 2 mm a year, as the Indian plate keeps pushing beneath. Why this peak and not a neighbour is probably "just luck".

Pull the plates ⇄The Indian plate broke from Pangaea and drove north for about 80 million years before it struck Asia.

Collision began 50–60 million years ago; the plates still converge at 40–50 mm a year.
